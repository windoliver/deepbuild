## What passes at the base, and why it should

No test passes. Inside the failing tests these lines ran before the failure and passed, each as it should, since each
reads what the base already has and none reads `scene.relations`:

- ac2-lamp.test.ts:45 (now :24), the one judgement that passes: the `geometry.audit` row of the room whose lamp stands on the
  floor is PASS with no error and no warning. The criterion says so itself ("while the geometry audit passes that
  room"): the audit is the base's own check kind, nothing in that room passes into anything or floats, and the
  definition declares the floor floating. That this row passes while `lamp on table` can't be judged is the gap the
  issue names.
- `presented`, on every fixture a run showed: #1732's room (ac1); the placed room with the lamp on the floor and
  with it lifted (ac2); the turned chairs, the rug across the wall and the crates (ac3); the books (ac4); the tipped
  chair (ac5); poses 0 to 99, whose `arrange@1` places nothing (ac6); the 1,000-item scene (ac7). Each is the base's
  own kinds, shown with no error issue and no item skipped.
- ac1-room.test.ts `solved`: `deep_query` gives each of the room's eight pieces three finite numbers for `at/pos` and
  `at/rot`, as #1732's suite reads the solve.
- ac6-measure.test.ts:50 and :52 (now :22 and :24): the reply's `audit.failures` reads, each `relation` failure with its `a`, `rel`,
  `b`, `by` and `deg`; and it reports `apart` of 6 m as missed for each of poses 0 to 99. That is the base's scene
  audit and the solve's own measure, the side of the comparison the base already has.
- ac7-budget.test.ts:50 (now :29): the check's `expect` holds 64 relations.
