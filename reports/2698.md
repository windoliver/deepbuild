## Needs your decision

**The perf lane should read FAIL for `hub-idle` against its merge base. It was not run, so this is read from `scripts/benchmark.mjs`'s `compare`.** The 15 s to settle and the 20 s span take the task from about 8 s to about 36 s, past the 50% margin on p50. `deep-status-30` and `deep-mcp-30d` now do more work too, so their instruction comparison will likely read above 2%. ## AC5: what failed and what changed

1. **First run of the test, 5 s span, load 197 to 216: fail.** The two `hub-idle` runs read 30.6 M and 6.9 M instructions a second, 342.6% apart. [Output](2698/ac5-change.txt).
2. **Second run, 5 s span, after the merge: pass.** Nothing had changed.
3. **Eight hubs read once a second** ([traces](2698/idle-traces-eight.txt), a diagnostic, not a test) showed why a 5 s span can't hold 10%:
   - A settled hub retires 5 to 13 M instructions in a second. Over seconds 16 to 20, two of the seven neighbouring pairs of runs were more than 10% apart, the widest 21.6%.
   - Start-up ends with a burst of about 500 M instructions centred 8 s after the core's ready line, in all ten hubs traced. It was over 4 to 5 s before the window at loads up to 300. A later start would put it in the window.
   - A hub does about 40 M of extra work once, 47 s after its socket answers.
4. **The change:** the reader's span is 20 s, from second 15 to second 35. That averages the second-to-second spread and ends before second 47. `idleCpuPercent` keeps its 5 s.
5. **Eight runs of the task after the change**, loads 49 to 99: 6.23 to 6.85 M a second. No neighbouring pair was more than 10% apart, and of all 28 pairs the widest was 9.9%. [Output](2698/idle-eight-runs.txt).
6. **Then the test, once: pass.**

The rule for steps 5 and 6 was written into the run script before it ran: `Closes` only if the test passed and at most one neighbouring pair was over 10%.

What is still not shown:
- **What the first run's extra work was.** A start-up burst reaching the window is the likely cause; it was not caught in a trace.
- **Loads above 300.** At a load near 600, with the 5 s span, the window read 84 M a second. The 15 s to settle is counted from the socket's answer and is the criterion's own number, so a slow enough start will still reach the span.
- **The margin.** 9.9% against a limit of 10% will fail now and then.

## Choices the issue left open

- **Wakeups** are the kernel's interrupt wakeups. Package idle wakeups are a subset of them and read 0 here.
- **Children** are direct children. A grandchild is not read.
- **A child that ended inside the span** is left out whole: its work before it ended is in no sum.
- **`deep-status-30`'s counted run is a second `deep status`** in the same round, not the timed one, so `statusMs` is measured as before.
- **`deep-mcp-30d`'s rounds** are the one round it times. Its instructions are `deep mcp`'s over that round, not from its start.
- **`hub-idle`'s span** for the reader is 20 s. Its `ps` CPU reading keeps the 5 s it had. #2676 plans 60 s.
- **`hub-cold-start`** reads the hub from its start to just after the socket answers. The hub works on between the answer and the reading, a few ms.
- **The reader's binary** is built once a machine into `~/Library/Caches/deepbuild-perf/`, beside the perf lane's counter, when a benchmark loads.
- **`scripts/lanes.mjs`** lists `tests/acceptance/2698/` as alone, as #1405's folder is: each file reads live processes under load. The entry sits on an existing line, so the file is no longer than main's.
